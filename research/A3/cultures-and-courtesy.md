# Courtesy across cultures, legitimate variation, religious practice (3.1, 3.3)

Agent A3, accessed 2026-11-08. Items: ACB-03-001, ACB-03-002, ACB-03-003, ACB-03-007, ACB-03-008, ACB-03-009

## Sources read

- **UNESCO Universal Declaration on Cultural Diversity (2001)** (UNESCO (text hosted by University of Minnesota Human Rights Library)): https://hrlibrary.umn.edu/instree/culturaldiversity.html
  - Supports: Cultural diversity is the common heritage of humanity, and no one may invoke it to infringe human rights.
  - Quote: "No one may invoke cultural diversity to infringe upon human rights."
- **Knigge am Arbeitsplatz Schweiz: Unternehmenskultur** (ibani.com (business etiquette guide)): https://www.ibani.com/de/leitfaden/knigge-arbeitsplatz-schweiz-unternehmenskultur.md
  - Supports: Swiss workplace etiquette: formal Sie by default until the senior colleague offers Du; punctuality (arriving exactly on the hour counts as late); in the Romandie 'vous' with first name is a legitimate local practice; in Ticino Lei remains the norm.
  - Quote: "vous paired with a first name is a legitimate local practice"
- **Ramadan and children** (Health for Kids (UK)): https://www.healthforkids.co.uk/grownups/?p=3459
  - Supports: Ramadan is the ninth month of the Islamic calendar; Muslims do not eat or drink during daylight; children are not required to fast until puberty (usually around 14); younger children often fast partially or build up gradually.
  - Quote: "Ramadan is the ninth month of the Islamic calendar."
- **Religion am Arbeitsplatz: Beten, Fasten, Kopfbedeckung und mehr** (Swissmem): https://www.swissmem.ch/de/wissen/personalwesen/arbeitsrecht/religion-am-arbeitsplatz-beten-fasten-kopfbedeckung-und-mehr.html
  - Supports: Art. 20a ArG: employees may take time off on religious holidays not recognised by the canton with at least three days' notice; the employer may order compensation of the lost working time; no individual right to prayer at set times; fasting employees deserve reasonable consideration.
  - Quote: "Die Arbeitgeberin darf einen entsprechenden Ausgleich der ausfallenden Arbeitszeit anordnen."
- **Feiertage im Arbeitsrecht** (arbeitsrecht-aktuell.ch): https://www.arbeitsrecht-aktuell.ch/de/2022/07/13/feiertage-im-arbeitsrecht/
  - Supports: Employees may stop work on religious holidays not recognised by the cantons, including non-Christian holidays; such days count legally as ordinary working days and are unpaid unless made up.
  - Quote: "Somit sind auch nicht-christliche Feiertage von dieser Regelung erfasst."

## Notes

Facts relied on: cultural diversity is a common heritage but cannot excuse rights violations (UNESCO 2001); Swiss workplace etiquette (Sie by default, strict punctuality, regional variation); Swiss Labour Act Art. 20a on religious holidays not recognised by the canton (three days' notice, compensation, unpaid unless made up); Ramadan basics and children (fasting not required before puberty).
Pitfalls: the Ramadan source is a UK children's-health page; puberty age (~14) varies by school of thought. Swissmem and arbeitsrecht-aktuell do not quote Art. 20a text itself; the item advises checking contract/labour office.
